# Child Safety Standards

**TCS — Taylors College Social**
**Operated by Lumora (Matthew Yiga), Sydney, NSW, Australia**
*Last updated: 27 May 2026*

TCS — Taylors College Social ("TCS", "the app", "we", "us") has a **zero-tolerance policy toward child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) and child sexual abuse material (CSAM)**. TCS is a closed community app for the students and staff of Taylors College Sydney. Because our community can include students who are minors (under 18), protecting children is a core responsibility, not an afterthought.

These standards explain how we prevent, detect, and respond to CSAE and CSAM on TCS, how users can report concerns, and how to contact us. They are published in line with Google Play's Child Safety Standards policy and are informed by the Tech Coalition's best practices for combating online child sexual exploitation and abuse.

---

## 1. Definitions

- **CSAE (child sexual abuse and exploitation):** any content or behaviour that sexually exploits, abuses, or endangers a child — including grooming a child for sexual exploitation, sextortion, trafficking, or otherwise sexually exploiting a minor.
- **CSAM (child sexual abuse material):** any visual depiction — including photos, videos, and computer- or AI-generated imagery — involving a minor engaged in sexually explicit conduct. CSAM is illegal and is absolutely prohibited on TCS.

---

## 2. Our prohibited-conduct standards

The following are strictly prohibited on TCS and are grounds for immediate account termination and referral to law enforcement:

- Creating, uploading, sharing, requesting, or linking to CSAM in any form, including AI-generated imagery.
- Grooming, soliciting, or attempting to sexually exploit a minor.
- Sextortion, sexual harassment, or sexualised communication directed at a minor.
- Sharing personal contact details of a minor for the purpose of exploitation, or attempting to move a minor to another platform for such purposes.
- Any other content or behaviour that sexually exploits, abuses, or endangers a child.

These prohibitions are part of our Terms of Service and Community Guidelines and apply to every user regardless of role.

---

## 3. How TCS is designed to protect children

Child safety is built into the structure of the app:

- **Closed, verified community.** TCS is not open to the public. Accounts can only be created for individuals whose details are pre-loaded from official Taylors College Sydney records, and login is verified against those records. There are no anonymous or open sign-ups.
- **Separation of students and staff.** Students and staff cannot search for one another, cannot follow one another, and **cannot exchange private one-to-one messages.** Interaction between students and staff happens only in visible, shared spaces such as clubs and study groups — never in private adult-to-minor channels. This separation is enforced on our server and cannot be bypassed by the app.
- **Consent-based contact.** A user must accept a chat request before another user can message them, preventing unsolicited contact.
- **Content moderation.** User-generated content (posts, comments, messages, uploaded photos and files, club content, and AI-generated images) is subject to our Community Guidelines, and we act on reports and on any content we become aware of.
- **AI-generated content safeguards.** Our AI features prohibit prompts or outputs that depict or sexualise minors. Misuse of AI features to attempt to generate such content is a serious violation and results in account termination and referral to authorities.

---

## 4. In-app reporting mechanism

Users can report child-safety concerns **without leaving the app.** Every post, profile, comment, and chat message includes a **Report** option, and reports can be flagged specifically as a child-safety or illegal-content concern. Users may also raise concerns through the in-app **Suggestion Box / feedback** channel. Reports are received and reviewed directly by the TCS operator.

Reports of suspected CSAE or CSAM are treated as the highest priority and are actioned urgently.

---

## 5. How we respond to CSAM and CSAE

When we obtain actual knowledge of CSAM or CSAE on TCS — whether through a user report or otherwise — we take prompt, appropriate action in accordance with these standards and applicable law, including:

1. **Removing** the offending content from the app as quickly as possible.
2. **Disabling** the account(s) responsible.
3. **Preserving** relevant data and evidence to the extent required to support investigation and reporting.
4. **Reporting** the matter to the relevant authorities (see Section 6).
5. **Cooperating** with law enforcement and child-protection authorities.

---

## 6. Reporting to authorities

TCS complies with all relevant child-safety laws and reports CSAM and CSAE to the appropriate national and international authorities, including:

- **Australian Centre to Counter Child Exploitation (ACCCE)** — https://www.accce.gov.au (report at https://www.accce.gov.au/report)
- **Australian Federal Police (AFP)** and relevant state police.
- **eSafety Commissioner (Australia)** — https://www.esafety.gov.au
- **National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) CyberTipline** (international) — https://report.cybertip.org

If you encounter CSAM or believe a child is in immediate danger, contact your local police or emergency services immediately. In Australia, call **000**.

---

## 7. Legal compliance

We comply with applicable child-safety laws in Australia and in the regions where TCS is available, and with the Australian Privacy Principles under the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth). We review these standards regularly and update them as our app, our practices, and the applicable law evolve.
