## 4. Community Separation Between Students and Teaching Staff

**TCS enforces a deliberate separation between student users and teaching staff users.** This is a core design principle of the app — not an optional setting — because TCS serves a school community where the relationship between students and staff carries particular professional and pastoral expectations.

The separation works as follows:

- **Feeds.** Students see content posted by other students. Teaching staff see content posted by other teaching staff. Cross-role posts are filtered out of feed responses at the server.
- **Follows.** Students cannot follow teaching staff, and teaching staff cannot follow students. Cross-role follow requests are rejected by the system with a clear message.
- **Search and suggested users.** Students do not appear in staff search results, and staff do not appear in student search results. The "Suggested users" feature only surfaces same-role peers.
- **Direct post access.** Attempting to open a cross-role post by direct link returns a "not found" response, preserving the separation even against URL guessing.

**Clubs are the one exception — they are the shared community space.**

- Both students and teaching staff may **join clubs** of any type.
- Posts made inside a club are visible to **all members of that club**, regardless of role.
- Club posts also appear in the main feed of every user (student or staff) who can see them under the normal club visibility rules.
- A staff member posting inside a club is acting in their capacity as a club member, and that post is shared with both student and staff club members.

We believe maintaining technological separation outside of clubs supports a healthy school community, while clubs preserve the option for staff and students to collaborate around shared interests and activities.

## 10. Wellbeing & AI Safety Support

To help the College support student wellbeing, TCS may include an automated wellbeing-support feature. **This feature is only active when your College has switched it on and has the necessary consent in place** (for example, consent obtained at enrolment from you or your parent/guardian).

**What it does.** When active, the messages you send to the in-app AI assistant are checked by an automated system for signs that you may be struggling or at risk (for example, language suggesting distress or self-harm). This is a triage signal that helps trained staff reach out — **it is not a diagnosis and never replaces a human or a crisis service.**

**What we store.** We do **not** store your chat transcripts for this purpose. When the system detects a possible concern it records only: a risk level, short non-identifying theme tags (e.g. "academic", "homesickness"), and a brief redacted snippet — together with the time and your account, so a staff member can follow up. Anonymous, aggregate trends may be shown to staff with no individual identified.

**Who can see it.** Identified wellbeing concerns are visible only to designated safeguarding staff at your College. Every time a staff member views or acts on a concern is recorded on an audit trail.

**Your choice.** You can turn this support **off for your own account at any time** in Settings → Wellbeing & Safety. If you turn it off, your messages are not checked or stored for wellbeing purposes. Turning it off does not affect your ability to use the rest of the app.

**In an emergency,** always contact a real crisis service — in Australia, Lifeline on **13 11 14** or **000** — rather than relying on the app.
